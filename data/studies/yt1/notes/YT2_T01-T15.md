# YT2 T01–T15 — TRADING RUSH single triggers (modules `tools/yt1/s_T01.py` … `s_T15.py`)

Each module is only `trigger(o, h, l, c, v)`; window, EMA200 side filter, swing stop, 1.5R target and fills are
`frame_t.py`. None of these fifteen has an own stop (`stop_l = stop_s = None`), so every trade is tagged `swing`.
"Crosses above x" is `ind.crossed_up` (above on this bar, at or below on the previous bar); a bar without an
indicator value (warm-up, or a 0 / 0) can neither signal nor serve as the previous bar of a cross.
Shorts are the exact mirror. All readings below were fixed before the first run.

## Per rule: formula coded, reading, check

**T01 — RSI(14) crosses 50.** `ind.rsi(c, 14)` (ta.rsi: Wilder averages, alpha 1/14, of the up and the down
close-to-close changes, each seeded with the SMA of its first 14 values; 100 − 100 / (1 + up / down)). Long: crosses
above 50; short: crosses below 50. No reading. Check: a from-scratch Wilder loop over the closes agrees on every bar
(max difference 0).

**T02 — Awesome Oscillator crosses zero.** AO = SMA5(hl2) − SMA34(hl2), hl2 = (high + low) / 2. Coded over one
denominator, (34 × S5 − 5 × S34) / 170 with S5 / S34 the 5- and 34-bar sums of hl2, so the sums are exact (see
"Arithmetic"). No reading. Check: direct 5- and 34-bar means at 500 bars agree to 2e-12; the zero cross agrees with
integer arithmetic (prices in eighths of a point) on every bar of the 3-, 5- and 15-minute series.

**T03 — AO below zero, third consecutive rising bar.** A bar is rising when AO > the previous AO (TradingView's green
bar) and falling when AO < it. Long at bar k: AO[k] < 0; bars k, k−1, k−2 rising; bar k−3 not rising
(AO[k−3] ≤ AO[k−4], so AO[k−4] must exist). Short: AO[k] > 0; three falling bars; bar k−3 not falling. Readings:
"below zero" is tested on the signal bar only; an unchanged AO counts as "not rising" for the long and as "not
falling" for the short. Check: integer arithmetic on every bar, 0 mismatches.

**T04 — CMO(9) crosses −50 (short: crosses below +50).** d = close − previous close; su = sum over the last 9 bars of
the d ≥ 0, sd = sum of −d over the d < 0; CMO = 100 × (su − sd) / (su + sd). Nine unchanged closes give 0 / 0 = no
value, as on TradingView. No reading. Check: a 9-bar loop at 500 bars (difference 0) and the cross in integer
arithmetic on every bar (0 mismatches).

**T05 — CMO(9) crosses 0.** Same CMO (`s_T04.cmo`); long crosses above 0, short crosses below 0. Same checks.

**T06 — Williams %R(14) crosses −80 (short: crosses below −20).** %R = 100 × (close − HH) / (HH − LL), HH / LL =
highest high / lowest low of the last 14 bars including the current one (range −100 … 0); HH = LL gives no value.
No reading. Check: max / min over 14-bar slices at 500 bars (difference 0); the cross in integer arithmetic on every
bar (0 mismatches).

**T07 — TRIX(18) crosses zero.** TradingView's built-in: TRIX = 10000 × (E3 − previous E3), E3 = EMA18(EMA18(EMA18(
ln close))), each EMA being ta.ema (alpha 2/19, seeded with the SMA of its first 18 values). Reading: the spec says
"1-bar % change of a triple EMA of log close"; TradingView takes the plain 1-bar change of that log series, which is
the % change to first order and has the same sign, so the zero cross is the same. Check: three nested pure-Python EMA
loops from the first bar agree on every bar to 4e-11 (values are of order 0.1–1).

**T08 — PPO crosses zero.** PPO = 100 × (EMA12 − EMA26) / EMA26 on the close (TradingView "Price Oscillator",
exponential, 12 / 26). No reading. Check: pure-Python EMA loops, every bar, difference 0.

**T09 — Fisher Transform(9) crosses its trigger with both below zero.** TradingView's built-in, m = hl2, HH / LL =
highest / lowest m of the last 9 bars: value = 0.66 × ((m − LL) / (HH − LL) − 0.5) + 0.67 × previous value, clamped
to ±0.999 beyond ±0.99 (the clamped value is carried forward); fisher = 0.5 × ln((1 + value) / (1 − value)) + 0.5 ×
previous fisher; trigger line = previous bar's fisher; a missing "previous" is 0. Long: fisher[k] > fisher[k−1] and
fisher[k−1] ≤ fisher[k−2], with fisher[k] < 0 and fisher[k−1] < 0. Short: the mirror, both above zero. Readings:
"both below zero" = the Fisher line and its trigger line on the cross bar; a flat 9-bar hl2 window (HH = LL: 167 of
252,566 five-minute bars, all on ten dates in March 2020 with prices pinned, none closing inside the 09:35–15:00
window) gives no value on that bar and both recursions restart from 0 on the next, which is what Pine's na / nz() do. Check: a bar-by-bar Pine transcription with slices
agrees on every bar (difference 0, same bars without a value).

**T10 — RVI(10) crosses its signal with both below zero.** TradingView's built-in: swma(x) = (x[k−3] + 2x[k−2] +
2x[k−1] + x[k]) / 6; RVI = (10-bar sum of swma(close − open)) / (10-bar sum of swma(high − low)); signal = swma(RVI).
Long: RVI crosses above the signal with RVI < 0 and signal < 0 on the cross bar. Short: RVI crosses below the signal
with both > 0. Reading: "both below zero" is tested on the cross bar. A zero denominator gives no value. Check: a
double loop at 1,000 bars (1e-16), and the whole trigger in exact fractions on every bar of all three timeframes
(0 mismatches).

**T11 — RVI(10) crosses its signal with both above zero.** Same RVI (`s_T10.rvi`). Long: crosses above with both
> 0; short: crosses below with both < 0. Same checks, 0 mismatches against exact fractions.

**T12 — Chaikin Money Flow(20) crosses zero.** TradingView's built-in: money-flow volume of a bar = ((2 × close − low
− high) / (high − low)) × volume, 0 when high = low; CMF = 20-bar sum of money-flow volume / 20-bar sum of volume.
Volume is the frame's `v` (the bar's summed 1-minute MNQ volume). No reading. Check: 20-bar loops at 500 bars
(1e-16); the sign of the numerator in exact fractions at 3,000 random and 3,000 signal bars per timeframe, 0
mismatches.

**T13 — Chaikin Oscillator crosses zero.** TradingView's built-in: EMA3(ADL) − EMA10(ADL), ADL (ta.accdist) = the
running sum from the first bar of the same money-flow volume as T12; both EMAs are ta.ema. Reading: none; the running
sum starts at the first bar of the data, and where it starts does not matter (restarting it 20,000 bars before a
bar moves the oscillator by 3e-9 against a typical size of 486). Check: pure-Python running sum and EMA loops, every
bar, difference 0.

**T14 — Close above the high of the latest confirmed up fractal, first bar to do so.** Up fractal at bar f:
high[f] strictly above high[f−2], high[f−1], high[f+1] and high[f+2]; it exists for the code only from bar f+2
(`confirmed_fractal` returns it at index f+2 and reads nothing later). Level at bar k = the high of the most recent
fractal confirmed at or before k. Long on the first bar, from the confirmation bar on, whose close is above that
level. Short: the mirror with down fractals (a low strictly below the two lows on each side) and a close below.
Readings: (1) the spec's own words ("a high above the two highs before and the two after") are coded, strictly on
both sides; TradingView's built-in fractal also accepts up to four equal highs to the left of the pivot, which would
add about 4 % more fractals (33,354 against 31,940 up fractals on the five-minute series; counted only, not run).
(2) "First bar to do so" = the first close above that fractal's high since it was confirmed: one signal per fractal
at most, later closes above the same level do not signal again, and a newer confirmed fractal replaces the level
even if the older one was never broken. Check: a backward scan per bar (find the latest fractal by direct
comparisons, then test every close since its confirmation) at 1,000 bars: level and signal identical.

**T15 — Schaff Trend Cycle (10, 23, 50) crosses 25 (short: crosses below 75).** TradingView has no built-in STC; the
spec's line is Schaff's algorithm as the standard scripts code it: m = EMA23(close) − EMA50(close); f1 = 100 × (m −
lowest(m, 10)) / (highest(m, 10) − lowest(m, 10)), the previous f1 when that range is 0; pf = previous pf + 0.5 ×
(f1 − previous pf); f2 = the same 10-bar stochastic of pf, the previous f2 when its range is 0; STC = previous STC +
0.5 × (f2 − previous STC). Readings: "smoothed by 0.5" is that recursion with factor 0.5, each started at its first
input value; a missing previous f1 / f2 is 0; STC starts at the first bar where both 10-bar windows are full (bar
67) rather than at 0 from bar 0 as a Pine script would. Check: a bar-by-bar Pine-style transcription (0 from bar 0)
differs only within the first 250 bars after the start of the data (June 2019) and agrees on every later bar to
2e-11.

## Arithmetic (a coding fault found by the indicator check, fixed after the first run)
The first version took rolling sums as differences of a running total of non-grid numbers. For RVI that left about
1e-14 where the true value is exactly 0 (478 five-minute bars), so "RVI below zero" was decided by rounding noise on
those bars. It was found when one T11 trigger bar disagreed with the plain-loop comparison on 15-minute bars
and the module was then compared with an exact form. Fix, same formulas: RVI's two sums are now exact sums of tick-grid numbers (the ÷6 cancels) and the signal is written around
RVI[k] so that four equal values give RVI[k] itself; AO is written over one denominator from exact sums; CMF's
numerator is summed over its own window and redone in exact fractions when it is within 1e-9 of zero.
Before / after, in-sample: T02, T03, T12: trigger arrays identical on all three timeframes, results unchanged.
T11: 1 raw signal differs on 5-minute and 3 on 3-minute bars, all outside the 09:35–15:00 window; orders and trades
identical. T10: raw signals 14,128 → 14,120 long and 15,083 → 15,077 short on 5-minute bars; candidate orders base
2,509 → 2,507, nb1 4,321 → 4,320, nb2 801 → 801; the two dropped base orders (the 2020-07-29 14:15 and 2021-11-16
13:40 bars, RVI exactly 0) fell while a position was open, so the trades are identical: base n 1752, R −0.045, p 0.9453 before and after (nb1 2916 / −0.076, nb2 632 / +0.072 likewise).
No other change was made after the first run.

## How the indicators were checked
1. Plain Python loops over the raw bar lists (no numpy arithmetic, no `ind.py`), written separately from the
   modules: the whole series for the recursive indicators (RSI, TRIX, PPO, Fisher, Chaikin oscillator, STC), 500 to
   1,000 bars for the windowed ones, on 5- and 15-minute bars. Largest difference 4e-11; no bar with a value on one
   side and none on the other. The trigger was re-evaluated from the loop values at 200 random bars and 300 signal
   bars per rule: 0 mismatches, except one T11 bar on 15-minute bars (2020-03-09 08:00, a limit-down morning where
   RVI equals its signal exactly and the loop's own rounding breaks the tie; exact fractions agree with the module).
2. Exact arithmetic for the triggers whose inputs are on the tick grid (T02–T06 in integers, T10–T12 in fractions),
   every bar of the 3-, 5- and 15-minute series: 0 mismatches.
3. `run.py <ID> --check --cuts 8`: PASS for all fifteen (final code).
