# C09 - Stochastic + RSI + MACD

Module: `tools/yt1/s_C09.py`. Variants: `base` 5-minute, `nb1` 3-minute, `nb2` 15-minute bars (`bar` parameter).

## 1. Readings added beyond the spec text
- **Arming is a state on each bar's own values.** Any bar with %K < 20 and %D < 20 arms a long; any bar with %K > 80
  or %D > 80 disarms it. Mirror: both > 80 arm a short, either < 20 disarms it. The state runs on the continuous
  24-hour series and carries across days.
- **Order inside a bar:** the bar's Stochastic first arms / disarms, then the trigger is tested. So a bar can arm and
  trigger at its own close, and a bar that disarms cannot trigger.
- **Trigger bar** = RSI(14) > 50 and MACD > signal on this bar, and not both of them true on the previous bar ("one
  of those two became true on that bar"; both turning true together also counts). Short: RSI < 50 and MACD < signal.
- **"The first bar" uses the arming up.** The first trigger bar while armed is the signal of that arming wherever it
  falls: outside the 09:35 -> 15:00 window, while a position is open, or on a roll day, it is simply not traded, and
  a later trigger needs a new arming (both lines under 20 again). On 5-minute bars: 3,521 long / 4,171 short signals
  over all hours, 893 / 1,047 inside the window (1,934 candidate orders after the flat-bar check, 1,188 trades).
- **Signal window on the neighbours:** open time + N minutes within 09:35 ... 15:00 (3-minute bars opening 09:33 ...
  14:57, 15-minute 09:30 ... 14:45).
- **Swing stop** = lowest low (highest high) of bars k-9 ... k, 1 tick beyond. **Target** = close -/+ 1.5 x
  |close - stop|, `core.tick_round` nearest.
