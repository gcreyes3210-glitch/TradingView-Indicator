# K-Q78 - Q7 and Q8: exit bars that held both the stop and the target (`tools/yt1/k_Q78.py`)

Stated in advance (`YT11_SPEC.md`, Part 4, "For Q7 and Q8"): the brackets are 1.5 to 5 points, smaller than a typical
1-minute bar; a bar that holds both the stop and the target counts as a stop (house rule), so the two rules are
scored at their worst. To be reported with them: the share of trades whose exit bar held both prices, and the result
if every such trade had been a winner (the best case; the truth is between the two). No pass / fail.

Run: `python3 tools/yt1/run.py K-Q78 --phase is` -> `data/studies/yt1/is/K-Q78.json`.

## 1. Definitions (all fixed before the first run)
- For every variant of `s_Q7` and `s_Q8`: the rule's own trades (`module.trades`), nothing re-simulated. Stop and
  target are the order's own prices (`module.orders`, matched to the trade by its signal bar `i`; the trade dict
  carries the stop but not the target). Exit bar = the trade's `k`.
- **both** = the exit bar's low <= stop price <= high and low <= target price <= high.
- **net house** = the sum of the trades' net as scored.
- **net best case** = the same, with each `both` trade's net replaced by the target outcome under the house fills:
  (|target - entry reference price| - 1 tick on each of the two fills) x $2 - $2, entry reference price = fill less
  the 1-tick entry slippage = the signal bar's close. For Q7 base that is (4.00 - 0.50) x 2 - 2 = +$5.00 in place of
  -$6.00.
- Added as description: how the `both` bars opened. A bar opening at or beyond the stop took the stop first
  whatever followed, so counting it as a winner overstates the best case (7 of 1,443 in Q7 base); a bar opening at
  or beyond the target reached the target first (1 of 1,443). The headline figures follow the spec and count all of
  them as winners. Also: win rates under both scorings, by-year blocks.

## 2. Coding errors found after first seeing results
None. The module was not changed after its first run (one line was tidied before the first run so the orders are
built once instead of twice).

What the table says, in sample:
- **Q7 cannot be scored on 1-minute bars.** More than half of its trades end on a bar that holds both prices, and
  the share grows with the price level (16 % in 2019, 68 % in 2022). The house rule and the best case have opposite
  signs for base, nb1 and nb2, so the true result lies somewhere in a range that includes zero. Only `x4` (5.5 %
  both) is measured to within a useful width, and it loses under both scorings (-$7,330 to -$1,390).
- **Q8 is measured well enough**: 3.3 % of base trades are affected and the rule loses under both scorings in every
  variant.
