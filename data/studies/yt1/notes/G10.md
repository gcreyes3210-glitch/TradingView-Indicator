## 1. Readings (R) — all fixed before the first run
1. **Overnight window (R).** The 1-minute bars of the cash day's trading date (`ctx.tdate`: 18:00 belongs to the next
   day) stamped 18:00 … 08:29, the 08:29 bar included. Overnight high = highest high, overnight low = lowest low.
   (Not `days.onh / onl`, which run to 09:29.)
2. **"Continuous series" (R).** `tt.cisd` is run once over the whole 1-minute series as loaded, so the run a CISD
   closes through may begin before 09:30 (or the evening before); only the CISD bar itself must be 09:30 … 10:59.
3. **"Above the overnight high" (R)** is strict: protected low > overnight high (short: protected high < overnight
   low). The first qualifying bar of either side is the day's signal (a bar cannot qualify on both sides).
4. **"First 1-minute bar of the next clock hour" (R)** = the first existing bar with hh:00 ≤ time < hh+1:00, and the
   decision bar is the bar just before it. In-sample that is always the hh:00 bar after an hh−1:59 bar (0 exceptions).
5. **Cancel window (R).** Lows (highs for a short) of the bars after the signal bar up to and including the last bar
   before the hour's open; "at or below" is ≤. A signal on the 09:59 / 10:59 bar has no window. If only the hour's
   opening print itself is at / through the stop, `core.simulate` rejects the order (stop not beyond the fill): no
   trade. 0 such cases in-sample in any variant. In `retry` such a case would count as the first signal having been
   used (no retry).
6. **Stop and target (R).** Stop = protected low − 1 tick (protected high + 1 tick). Target = hour-bar open ±
   k × |open − stop|, on the open **before** slippage (YT1 common rule "R targets"; "from the fill price" read as "from
   the price the market order fills at", not from the signal bar's close), `core.tick_round` nearest (only 1.5R can
   fall between ticks).
7. **"An ORB trade entered earlier that day is still open" (R).** Entered = ORB's entry bar (the 1-minute bar at whose
   close it enters) is before the hour-open bar. That includes an ORB entry at the close of the 09:59 / 10:59 bar,
   the 5-minute close that coincides with the hour: 13 of the 83 base trades. Still open = ORB had not exited by the
   close of the decision bar; an ORB stopped inside the hour bar counts as open (0 base trades in-sample).
8. **Days not traded.** `core.run_orders(..., max_per_day=1, skip_roll=2)` for every variant, `signal`, `alone` and
   `naive` included. ORB itself is taken with `skip_roll=False` (it trades on roll days), exactly as `cal_orb` main.
9. **`retry` (R).** Only the day's **second** qualifying signal may replace the first (one retry), judged at its own
   hour with its own stop and cancel window. "Next" = next in time after the first signal's bar. Under a chained
   reading (third, fourth … signal) 83 in-sample days would have a later signal that passes; counted only, never run.
10. **`alone`.** The day's first signal, not cancelled, when ORB at that hour is anything but "open on the same side"
    (in-sample: not yet entered 129 days, entered and already out 2, open on the other side 0, before roll days).
11. **`naive` (R).** Hour from ORB's entry bar: before 10:00 → 10:00; 10:00 … 10:59 → 11:00; later → no trade. ORB must
    be open at that open. Order: market at that open on ORB's side, stop = ORB's stop price, no target, flat bar =
    **ORB's own flat bar** from `cal_orb.orders` (the 16:04 bar), so that the time exit is ORB's. ORB's realised exit
    bar is never put in the order.
12. The add's flat bar is the harness flat bar (`day.i_end`, 15:59 or early).

## 2. How ORB's state is used, and the fill-dependent target
- `prep(ctx)` simulates ORB once (`core.trades_df(core.run_orders(ctx, cal_orb.orders(ctx), skip_roll=False))`).
  `orb_state(o, i)` returns what is known at the close of the decision bar `i`: not entered (`j > i`), entered and
  out (`k <= i`), or open with its side. ORB's exit bar is used only through the test `k <= i`.
- The target is k R from the order's own fill = `open[i+1]`, which `core.causal_check` mirrors. The lab's `s_Z00.py`
  says only that an `'open'` order may read `ctx.O[i + 1]` for its own stop / target; `s_E08.py` shows the test to run
  in that case. What I did:
  - `orders(ctx, **p)` returns each order built from bars ≤ `i` only (side, stop, flat bar) and **no target**.
  - `full_orders(ctx, **p)` adds `target = tick_round(O[i+1] + side·k·|O[i+1] − stop|)`; this is the only read of a
    bar after `i`. `trades()` = `run_orders(ctx, full_orders(...), max_per_day=1, skip_roll=2)`.
  - `run.py G10 --check` and `core.causal_check` run on `orders()`; `check_true_open` (E08's test: the future is
    mirrored except the open of the first bar after the signal bar) runs on `full_orders()`, targets included.

- `python3 tools/yt1/s_G10.py` (log `logs/G10_lookahead.txt`), 16 samples each:

| Variant | `core.causal_check` on `orders()` | mirrored future with the fill bar's open kept, on `full_orders()` |

| naive | PASS | PASS (no target in this variant) |
| signal | PASS | PASS |

  `orders()` is the same list for base, nb1 and nb2 (k enters only in `full_orders`), so the first column tests the
  same thing three times; the second column is where the three targets are tested.
- For the record, the unmodified `core.causal_check` on `full_orders()` (base) FAILS on 13 of 16 samples, and in every
  one the only field that differs is the target of the sampled order itself (e.g. 2019-12-16: stop 8571.25 in both,
  target 8645.50 with the true 10:00 open, 8647.00 with the mirrored one); the 3 that pass are days where the hour
  bar opens at the previous close. Nothing else differs.
- Negative control: a deliberately leaky base that asks ORB to be still open 60 bars after the decision bar FAILS
  `core.causal_check`, so the test does see a use of ORB's realised exit.

## 6. Comparison with the first implementation (done after everything above was written)
File read: `data/studies/yt1/is/G9_base.csv` (207 in-sample trades of YT6 pick 1). `s_G9.py`, `g9_*.py` and
`notes/G9.md` were not opened: there was no difference to diagnose.

**(a) My `signal` against G9_base, by day.** 207 mine, 207 theirs; 207 matched, 0 only mine, 0 only theirs. All 207
are identical on signal-bar time, entry time, exit time, side, entry, stop, exit, P&L, R and exit reason (net
+7,966.50 both, sum of R 57.5144 both). No difference to explain.

**(b) My ORB condition applied to their trades, against my `base`.** For each of their trades: the decision bar is
the bar before their fill bar, and the trade is kept when `orb_state` at that bar is "open on the trade's side".
83 of their 207 are kept (net +5,116.50, 55.4 % wins, mean R +0.4328); my `base` has 83; 83 matched, 0 only mine,
0 only theirs, all identical on the same ten fields. Given (a), (b) tests that my `base` is exactly "the signal trade
kept when the ORB condition holds" and nothing else (no order is added or lost by filtering before the harness
instead of after); it is not an independent check of the ORB condition itself, since the same `orb_state` is on
both sides. The outside figure for that is the spec's own disclosure for 2019–2022, "55 % and +5,116 $", which my
`base` reproduces (55.4 %, +5,116.50).

**Where a different reading would have shown up.** Reading 7 (an ORB entry at the close of the 09:59 bar counts as
"entered earlier" for the 10:00 open) carries 13 of the 83 base trades (net +775.00, 6 wins); with ORB required to
have entered at least one bar earlier the list would be 70 trades, +4,341.50, which is not the spec's figure. No
code was changed after the comparison.
