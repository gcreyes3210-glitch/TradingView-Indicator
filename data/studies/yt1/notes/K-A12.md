# K-A12 - ICT opening-range gap to its midpoint, claim check (`tools/yt1/k_A12.py`)

Claim (ICT): "you're looking for 70% likelihood it's going to pull back into that opening range gap, 50% of it".
The gap runs from the previous day's settlement, read at 16:14 on the 1-minute chart, to the 09:30 open. He likes
"20 to 75 handles", calls 75 to 120 a grey zone and expects a gap over 120 to keep going. No sample, no contract or
price level for the handles, and no statement whether the 70 % is for 09:30-10:00 or the session. No pass / fail.

## 1. Definitions added beyond the spec text (all fixed before the first run)
- Settlement proxy = close of the 1-minute bar stamped 16:14 on the previous cash day (the row before in the day
  table). Left out and counted: 29 days whose previous cash day is a short session with no 16:14 bar, and 15 days on
  which the 09:30 bar is a different contract from that 16:14 bar (the roll days). 881 of 926 cash days measured.
- Gap = 09:30 bar's open - proxy (positive = premium, negative = discount); midpoint = their average, not rounded.
  It "trades" when a 1-minute bar of the window reaches it: low <= midpoint for a premium gap, high >= midpoint for
  a discount gap.
- Windows: 09:30-10:00 = bars 09:30 .. 09:59; "by 16:00" = bars 09:30 .. 15:59 (to the halt on the 24 short days
  measured).
- Buckets in points as spoken: 20 <= |gap| < 75; 75 <= |gap| <= 120; |gap| > 120. Gaps under 20, which he does not
  address, are shown too.
- The same thresholds as a share of price at 2025 levels: 20 / 75 / 120 points on 21,000 = 0.0952 % / 0.3571 % /
  0.5714 %, applied to |gap| / proxy. In-sample MNQ traded between about 6,800 and 16,800, so a "20 to 75 point"
  gap of 2019 is two to three times larger, relative to price, than the gap he is describing in 2025.

## 2. Coding errors found after first seeing results
None in the measure. After the first run the labels of the percentage buckets were rewritten so that they name the
rescaled thresholds (they first read "20 to 75" in both tables); every number was identical before and after.

## 3. Hand check
Five days drawn at random and recomputed from the 1-minute bars with pandas; all agree with the module:

Reading the number: for the gap size he likes, the midpoint trades in the first half hour 67 % of the time with his
point thresholds and 80 % with the thresholds scaled to price; by the close 81 % and 90 %. The ordering he
describes holds: the rate falls steadily with gap size, and gaps under his 20-point floor fill most often of all.
The measure has no direction or entry in it; whether a trade toward the midpoint pays is not answered here.
