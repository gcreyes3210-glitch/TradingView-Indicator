# K-E10 - first hour and 15:00 continuation, claim check (`tools/yt1/k_E10.py`)

Claims (edgeful videos, six-month samples): a green first hour -> the day closes above its open, 76-79 % on NQ; a
red one -> closes below, 72-72.5 % (82 % in a three-month sample); price above the day's open at 15:00 -> the day
closes above its open, about 90 % on YM, 91 % on ES when also above the middle of the day's range. No pass / fail:
measured next to claimed.

## 1. Definitions added beyond the spec text (all fixed before the first run)
- Day's open = the 09:30 bar's open. "10:30 price" = the close of the bar stamped 10:29; "15:00 price" = the close
  of the bar stamped 14:59; "16:00 close" = the close of the day's last 1-minute bar before 16:00 (on an early-close
  day, the last bar before its halt).
- Green = that price strictly above the day's open; red = strictly below; equal = flat, in neither group (one day,
  2020-02-14, for the first hour; none at 15:00).
- Two figures per group, each with its count:
  - **what the videos claim**: green -> 16:00 close above the day's open (red: below);
  - **what a trade could capture**: green -> 16:00 close above the 10:30 (15:00) price (red: below). A close equal
    to the signal price is not a hit; the number of such days is printed.
- The 15:00 rule is given twice: against the open alone, and with the range filter of E10b (also above / below the
  middle of the 09:30-14:59 high-low range).
- Every cash day of the day table is counted, roll days included (nothing here crosses a day). First-hour figures
  are also given without the 29 early-close / holiday sessions. The 15:00 figures need a 14:59 bar inside the
  session: the 29 short sessions and 2019-06-18 (no 14:59 bar) drop out, 896 days.
- "Any day" lines are the same outcome with no signal, for reference. The claimed figure for red days at 15:00 is
  not stated in the videos.

## 2. Coding errors found after first seeing results
None. The module was not changed after its first run.

## 3. Hand check
- Six days read off the 1-minute bars and compared with the module's rows: 2019-10-21 (open 7914.00, 10:30 price
  7921.75 green, close 7946.50: above the open and above the 10:30 price), 2022-02-25 (13995.25, 13991.25 red,
  close 14182.00: fails both), 2021-08-18 (14978.75, 15017.50 green, 15:00 price 14988.50, close 14856.00: fails
  all), 2022-09-30 (green first hour, red at 15:00, close 11028.75), 2019-11-29 (early close: last bar 13:14, no
  15:00 row), 2020-02-14 (10:30 price equal to the open: flat). All agree.
- Whole sample recounted a second way (pandas `between_time` and group-by, no shared code), days without an early
  close: first hour green 466 -> 342 above the open, 281 above the 10:30 price; red 430 -> 290, 219; 15:00 green
  492 -> 437, 261; red 404 -> 359, 219. Identical to the module.
- The groups are the trade rules' days: 482 + 443 first hours = E10a's 911 orders plus the 14 days without a daily
  ATR; 460 + 326 at 15:00 with the range filter = E10b's 786 orders.

Reading the numbers. The published rates reproduce in-sample to within a few points (the first-hour ones 2 to 5
points under the claim, the 15:00 ones on it). They are high because the day is already on that side of its open
when the signal is read: the close only has to not give the move back. Measured from the price at which a trade
would be entered, the same days close further in the signal's direction 51-60 % of the time. For comparison, any
day closes above its 10:30 price 54.8 % of the time and above its 15:00 price 49.4 %, so the signal adds roughly 4
to 6 points over no signal at all (green first hour 60.2 % against 54.8 %; red 51.2 % against 45.2 %; at 15:00,
53-56 % against about 50 %). These are frequencies, not profits: E10a and E10b put fills and costs on the same
days.
