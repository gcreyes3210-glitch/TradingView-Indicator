# E13 - Crabel stretch after a 2-day narrow range (`tools/yt1/s_E13.py`)

## 1. Readings added beyond the spec text (all fixed before the first run)
- "RTH days" = the rows of the harness day table (cash sessions; holiday short sessions are rows too). Set-up and
  stretch use COMPLETED previous days only: on day t the highs, lows and 09:30 opens of rows t-1, t-2, ... from
  `ctx.days` (`rth_h`, `rth_l`, `o930`); today's own high and low are never read. Today's 09:30 open is used for
  the levels (known at the signal bar).
- N-day range ending on day s = max(high of s-N+1..s) - min(low of s-N+1..s). Set-up on day t: the range ending on
  t-1 is <= every N-day range lying wholly inside the last 20 completed days t-20..t-1 (19 two-day windows; `nr3`:
  18 three-day windows). An equal range elsewhere does not cancel the set-up. The first 20 days have no set-up.
  Nothing is adjusted across a contract roll: a window spanning a roll carries the roll jump and is simply wider.
- Stretch on day t = mean over rows t-10..t-1 of min(09:30 open - RTH low, RTH high - 09:30 open).
- Signal bar = the 09:30 bar. Levels off the tick grid: buy stop rounded up, sell stop rounded down (the first tick
  an order at the exact level would trade at). Both rest from the 09:31 bar through the last bar before 15:00.
- "The other level is its stop": the protective stop is the other order's price exactly (no extra tick). No target;
  flat bar. R = |fill - that stop|.
- First to fill: `orders()` returns both candidates; `trades()` simulates each and keeps the earlier fill bar (both
  in the same 1-minute bar = no trade; it does not occur in-sample), dropping roll and no-ATR days itself.
- Break-even *(the spec's own R)*: with the fill in the bar stamped t, `trail[k]` = the entry price for every bar k
  from the first bar stamped t + 60 minutes or later, NaN before; the harness applies it from the following bar.
  "Entry price" = the fill before slippage (the harness's own break-even convention). The trail is built from the
  fill time alone: a first simulation finds the fill bar, a second one runs the same order with the trail.
- Consequence of that last line as written: if price is on the losing side of the entry when the 60 minutes are
  up, the stop has moved through the market and the trade is closed at the next bar's open.
