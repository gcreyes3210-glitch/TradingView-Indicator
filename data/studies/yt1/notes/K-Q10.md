# K-Q10 - the mean MNQ return of each of the 24 clock hours (`tools/yt1/k_Q10.py`)

`YT11_SPEC.md`, Q10, "Reported: ... the mean MNQ return of each of the 24 clock hours, in sample and on the full
span (description)". Published on ES (N06, Boyarchenko, Larsen and Whelan, 1998-2020, Table I, basis points per
day before costs): 00:00-01:00 +0.46 (t 2.8), 01:00-02:00 +0.43 (t 2.8), 02:00-03:00 +1.5 (t 7.1; 1.48 in the
introduction), 17:00-18:00 -0.43 (t -3.6), "the other hours are flat". No pass / fail: measured next to published.

Run: `python3 tools/yt1/run.py K-Q10 --phase is` -> `data/studies/yt1/is/K-Q10.json`.

## 1. Definitions (fixed before the first run, except where section 2 says otherwise)
- **Hour H** (0 ... 23) = from the close of the bar stamped (H-1):59 to the close of the bar stamped H:59, New York
  wall clock; hour 0 starts at the 23:59 bar of the previous calendar date. Hour 2 is Q10's hour. Both bars must
  exist: a day on which either minute has no bar is not counted for that hour.
- **Which days.** An hour belongs to the trading day of its H:59 bar (18:00 and later belong to the next day).
  Counted: trading days that are cash days of the day table, **roll days excluded** (the contract changes at 19:00
  or 20:00 on the evening before a roll day, inside that trading day). Days without a daily ATR are kept (nothing
  here needs the ATR), so the hours have a few more days than Q10 has trades. The two bars must also be the same
  contract (never fails after the roll-day exclusion: 0 pairs).
- **Figures.** Return in basis points = (close / close - 1) x 10,000; in points = the difference of the closes; n =
  days counted; t = mean / (standard deviation / sqrt(n)); also the share of days up.
- **Hours 17 and 18.** MNQ is shut 17:00-18:00, so there is no 17:59 bar to end hour 17 or start hour 18. By the
  definition they are empty except for stray 17:59 bars (2020-03-31 and 2020-06-30 in sample: n 2 and n 1, not
  usable). Two rows outside the 24 stand in for them: `17_halt` = the close of the 16:59 bar to the open of the
  18:00 bar on the same calendar date (the daily halt, Monday to Thursday evenings; the weekend gap is not in it);
  `18_from_open` = the open of the 18:00 bar to the close of the 18:59 bar.

## 2. Changes after the first run
- No change to the 24 rows or to how days are chosen.
- The two rows outside the 24 were changed once, after the first output and before any other use: the first version
  had `18_from_open` and a row `reopen` = the 16:59 close to the 18:59 close (n 702, +1.416 bp, t 1.41). That row
  mixed the halt gap with the 18:00 hour, so it was replaced by `17_halt` (the gap alone), which with `18_from_open`
  splits the same stretch in two. A change of description, not of a tested figure.

## 3. Hand check
- Second route for all 24 hours (plain loops over calendar dates with `ctx.idx`, no vectorised lookup): n, mean in
  basis points, mean in points and t agree with the module for every hour.
- 2021-03-03, hour 2: 01:59 close 13146.25, 02:59 close 13170.50 -> +24.25 points = +18.446 bp; trading day
  2021-03-03.
- 2021-03-03, hour 0: 2021-03-02 23:59 close 13138.25, 00:59 close 13146.25 -> +8.00 points = +6.089 bp.
- 2021-03-03, hour 20: 19:59 close 12643.75, 20:59 close 12647.50 -> +3.75 points; the trading day of the 20:59 bar
  is 2021-03-04.
- Against Q10: hour 2 has 903 days at +1.403 points; Q10 base has 892 trades at a gross of +1.447 points. The 11
  extra days are the days without a daily ATR, which Q10 cannot trade.

Reading the table, in sample (911 days at most, 3.6 years):
- **02:00-03:00: +1.52 bp a day (t 1.9), against 1.5 published on ES.** The size reproduces; with 903 days the
  t-statistic is far below the paper's 7.1 (which has 23 years). In points it is +1.40 a day, and one round trip
  costs 1.5.
- 00:00-01:00 is the strongest night hour here (+1.92 bp, t 2.6; published +0.46); 01:00-02:00 +0.81 (published
  +0.43). The three hours together are the night's drift; `eu` (Q10) holds 23:30-03:30.
- No hour of the 24 reaches |t| = 3; with 24 hours, one or two at |t| about 2 is what chance gives. 14:00-15:00
  (+2.48 bp, t 1.9) and 07:00-08:00 (-1.55 bp, t -1.7) are the next largest.
- The lunch hours of Q11: 11:00-12:00 +0.83 bp, 12:00-13:00 -0.30, 13:00-14:00 -1.39: the opposite signs to the
  SPY pattern, none distinguishable from zero.
- The daily halt (`17_halt`) is +0.45 bp over 706 evenings. The published 17:00-18:00 figure for ES (-0.43) has no
  counterpart by the definition here: MNQ has no bars in that hour.
