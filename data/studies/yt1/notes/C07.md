# C07 - MACD + Parabolic SAR + 200 EMA

Module: `tools/yt1/s_C07.py` (parameters `bar` and `k_r`; base 5-minute 1.5R, nb1 5-minute 1R, nb2 5-minute 2R).

## 1. Readings added beyond the spec text (all fixed before the first run)
- MACD (12, 26, 9), the default the sourcing note gives. "Histogram crosses above zero" = `ind.crossed_up(hist, 0)`
  on completed bars: hist[k] > 0 and hist[k-1] <= 0.
- "SAR below the bar" = sar[k] < low[k]; "above the bar" = sar[k] > high[k]. The direction flag of `ind.psar` is
  not used, because on a bar where the SAR turns `ind.psar` can leave the dot inside the bar (section 3), and a dot
  inside the bar is not below it.
- "The SAR value" = sar[k] of the signal bar, fixed for the life of the trade, placed on the first tick at or
  beyond it (down for a long, up for a short): exactly the bars that touch the value touch that tick.
- Target = close +/- k_r x |close - stop| (the stop as placed), `core.tick_round` nearest.
- "A bar giving the opposite signal" = a bar on which the mirrored entry signal fires exactly as defined, the
  09:35 -> 15:00 window included (so after 15:00 only the stop, the target and the flat bar can close a trade).
  The trade is closed at that bar's close through `simulate`'s `exit_sig`; a stop or target touched in that bar
  comes first.
- **Not expressible as stop-and-reverse:** the opposite signal that closes a trade is not itself taken, because
  `run_orders` skips an order signalled at or before the previous trade's exit bar. The spec only says the trade
  "exits", so this is the nearest form and no work-around was written.
- "Bars closing 09:35 -> 15:00" = clock close in [09:35, 15:00].
- Decision bar `i` = `i_last` of the signal bar, as the brief defines it. Orders on a bar whose last minute has no
  1-minute bar: 1 of 1,950 (2020-03-16 09:30, the circuit-breaker open, which is also a roll day); it did not
  become a trade.

## 2. Coding errors found after first seeing results
None. Nothing was changed after the first run.

## 3. Checks
Indicators (once, on the 5-minute series): `ind.ema` and `ind.macd` equal independent pandas recursions exactly.

**`ind.psar` does not look like TradingView's `ta.sar`; reported here, not patched, and used as it is.**
Evidence that does not depend on any reference: after a 2,000-bar warm-up (250,566 bars), on 331 of the 10,818
bars where the direction turns up the dot is above the bar's low, and on 400 of the 10,818 turn-down bars it is
below the bar's high: the dot is inside the bar it is supposed to be outside of (on 78 and 104 of them it is even
past the close). On bars where the direction does not turn, the dot is never inside the bar. Example: 2020-10-20
09:30, a turn-down bar with high 11702.00, `ind.psar` 11691.00.
Compared with my transcription of the `pine_sar` reference that TradingView publishes for `ta.sar` (written from
memory; the reference page could not be read from here and the web-search allowance was used up, so the
transcription itself is unverified): the direction differs on 6,941 bars (2.77 %), and with the same direction the
value differs on 25,929 more. Two differences in the code explain it: (a) on a turn `ind.psar` sets the SAR to the
old extreme point, the reference to the farther of that and the turn bar's own high / low, so the reference's dot
is never inside the bar (0 such bars in the transcription); (b) `ind.psar` limits the new SAR to the previous two
lows / highs before testing for a turn (Wilder's order), the reference tests first and limits afterwards, so it
turns a little more often.
What it means for this rule: among the window bars with the MACD and EMA conditions true (1,280 long, 1,125
short), the SAR is beyond the bar on 1,049 / 901 with `ind.psar` and on 1,051 / 906 with the transcription; 1,038 /
888 are signals under both, and on 84 / 52 of those the SAR value (the stop) differs by more than a tick. So if the
transcription is right, an exact `ta.sar` would drop 24 of the 1,950 signals, add 31 others and move the stop of
about 7 % of the rest. No result was computed with the transcription.

Stops:
- orders whose stop is not on the losing side (rejected by the harness): 0 of 1,950 signals (the SAR must be beyond
  the bar, so it is beyond the close);
- would-be signals removed by "below the bar" where the direction flag alone would have let them through (flag on
  the trade's side, dot at or inside the bar's extreme): 51. On none of them is the dot on the wrong side of the
  close, so with the flag reading they would have been orders with a stop inside the signal bar, not rejections.
Exits in base: 455 stop, 371 target, 332 flat bar, 95 opposite signal.
No trade on a roll day or the day after; no overlapping trades; every trade flat the same day.
Look-ahead test: `run.py C07 --check` PASS (16 of 16, the `exit_sig` arrays included); the same test on nb1 and nb2
(8 samples each) also passes.
